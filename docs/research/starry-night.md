# The Starry Night — verified research

Painting: *The Starry Night*, Vincent van Gogh, Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, June 1889.
Collection: The Museum of Modern Art, New York (MoMA).
Room: **Wonder**. Storytelling mode: **authentic letters**.

This file is the gate (CLAUDE.md rule 10): no quote or factual claim may appear in the
experience unless it is listed here as **cleared**. Anything under "Not cleared" must not
ship until it is re-verified.

## How this was verified — method and one honest limitation

The scholarly source of record for letter wording and numbering is **vangoghletters.org**
(*Vincent van Gogh — The Letters*, 2009, Van Gogh Museum & Huygens ING; eds. Jansen,
Luijten, Bakker). Letter numbers below are that edition's numbering.

**Limitation:** this build environment's network policy blocks direct page loads of
vangoghletters.org (and web.archive.org). Wording below was captured from the search
index's excerpts of the vangoghletters.org letter pages themselves (searches scoped to
that domain), cross-checked against MoMA's catalog text where it quotes the same letters.
Every entry lists its canonical URL. **Reviewer checklist: open each URL and compare the
quote character-for-character before any of these words ship in the experience.**

## Verified facts about the painting

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Painted at the Saint-Paul-de-Mausole asylum, Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, where Van Gogh spent twelve months in 1889–90; the canvas dates to mid-June 1889. | MoMA, catalog entry: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/79802 |
| F2 | Van Gogh announced it to Theo as "a new study of a starry sky" alongside a landscape with olive trees, in a letter of on or about 18 June 1889. | Letter 782: https://vangoghletters.org/vg/letters/let782/letter.html |
| F3 | The night vision was observed from his upstairs window, but the painting was made in several **daytime** sessions (his studio was elsewhere in the building); it mixes direct observation with imagination, memory, and emotion. | MoMA, catalog entry: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/79802 |
| F4 | The village below the hills **could not be seen from his window** — it is based on other views and invention. | MoMA Learning: https://www.moma.org/learn/moma_learning/vincent-van-gogh-the-starry-night-1889/ |
| F5 | The church steeple resembles those of his native Holland, not of Provence. | MoMA Learning (as F4) |
| F6 | The brightest orb left of center is **Venus, the morning star**, genuinely visible and prominent in the pre-dawn sky in spring 1889. | MoMA catalog entry (as F1); corroborated by letter 777 below |
| F7 | His window was iron-barred; through it he saw an enclosed square of wheat and the sunrise (his own words — see Q5). | Letter 776: https://vangoghletters.org/vg/letters/let776/letter.html |

## Verified quotes (cleared for use, pending reviewer's URL check)

Rules of use (CLAUDE.md 9, 11): sparing, surfacing like remembered thought — never
narration, never a wall of text. Any elision must be shown with an ellipsis; never
splice two letters into one sentence.

### Q1 — the stars as need (sky discovery node)
> "That doesn't stop me having a tremendous need for, shall I say the word — for
> religion — so I go outside at night to paint the stars, and I always dream a painting
> like that, with a group of lively figures of the pals."

- Letter **691**, to Theo van Gogh. Arles, on or about Saturday, 29 September 1888.
- https://vangoghletters.org/vg/letters/let691/letter.html
- Note: written in **Arles, nine months before** *The Starry Night* — it documents a
  longing that preceded the painting, and must not be presented as written at Saint-Rémy.
- The popular variant "When I have a terrible need of — shall I say the word — religion —
  then I go out and paint the stars" (used in MUSEUM_BIBLE.md) is an **older loose
  translation. Use the wording above.** If shortened for the experience, use:
  "…a tremendous need for, shall I say the word — for religion — so I go outside at
  night to paint the stars…"

### Q2 — the stars as map (horizon/star discovery node)
> "…the sight of the stars always makes me dream in as simple a way as the black spots
> on the map, representing towns and villages, make me dream."

- Letter **638**, to Theo van Gogh. Arles, Monday, 9 or Tuesday, 10 July 1888.
- https://vangoghletters.org/vg/letters/let638/letter.html
- The popular variant "Looking at the stars always makes me dream…" is again a different
  translation; use the wording above.

### Q3 — death as a train to the stars (same letter; reserve, use with care)
> "Just as we take the train to go to Tarascon or Rouen, we take death to go to a star."

- Letter **638** (same as Q2).
- Emotional-ceiling caution (rule 15): this line is serene in context but reads heavier
  out of context. If used at all, it belongs mid-experience, never as a closing note.

### Q4 — the cypresses (cypress discovery node)
> "The cypresses still preoccupy me, I'd like to do something with them like the
> canvases of the sunflowers because it astonishes me that no one has yet done them as
> I see them."

- Letter **783**, to Theo van Gogh. Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, Tuesday, 25 June 1889 —
  written **days after** the canvas.
- https://vangoghletters.org/vg/letters/let783/letter.html
- Reveal it supports: he saw the cypress as alive and worthy of devotion — not the
  graveyard death-symbol of tradition.
- The bible's paraphrase "The cypresses are always occupying my thoughts…" is the older
  translation; use the wording above.

### Q5 — the barred window and the wheat (horizon node, environmental grounding)
> "Through the iron-barred window I can make out a square of wheat in an enclosure, a
> perspective in the manner of Van Goyen, above which in the morning I see the sun rise
> in its glory."

- Letter **776**, to Theo van Gogh. Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, on or about Thursday,
  23 May 1889.
- https://vangoghletters.org/vg/letters/let776/letter.html

### Q6 — the morning star (Venus node)
> "This morning I saw the countryside from my window a long time before sunrise, with
> nothing but the morning star, which looked very big."

- Letter **777**, to Theo van Gogh. Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, between about Friday, 31 May
  and about Thursday, 6 June 1889 — weeks before the canvas.
- https://vangoghletters.org/vg/letters/let777/letter.html
- Wording corroborated by MoMA's catalog entry, which quotes this line.

## Not cleared — do not ship without re-verification

- **Letter 783's cypress description beyond Q4** (the sentence likening the cypress to
  an Egyptian obelisk, and the "dark patch in a sun-drenched landscape" phrase). Two
  renderings circulate ("It's as beautiful of line and proportion as an Egyptian
  obelisk" vs. "beautiful as regards lines and proportions, like an Egyptian obelisk")
  and I could not pin the edition's exact sentence from here. Verify on the letter page
  before using any of it.
- **Any claim that the painting depicts the view through bars, or was painted at
  night.** Both are false (F3; the sky was watched from the barred bedroom window, the
  painting made by day elsewhere in the building). The bible's "faint suggestion of bars
  at the edge of vision" motif must stay a *felt* shift and must never assert bars are
  in the painting.
- **Any inner-monologue paraphrase** of Van Gogh not in the letters ("I dream my
  painting…" etc. — apocryphal). Only the quotes above, only verbatim.

## Emotional-ceiling guidance (rule 15)

The confinement is real and belongs in the experience — as ground under the wonder, not
as the conclusion. The arc of fact is genuinely upward: the same window that had bars
(Q5) is where he watched Venus rise looking very big (Q6), and what he made of it is the
sky everyone now knows. Close on the sky, never on the asylum.
