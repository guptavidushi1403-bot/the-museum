# Water Lilies / Grandes Décorations — verified research

Painting: the *Grandes Décorations* water-lilies cycle, Claude Monet (installed Musée de
l'Orangerie, Paris, 1927); the wider *Nymphéas* series painted at Giverny.
Room: **Serenity**. Storytelling mode: **documented intention** (reported statements —
Monet left no confessional letters like Van Gogh's; his intent survives chiefly through
interviews and documented remarks).

This file is the gate (CLAUDE.md rule 10): nothing ships that isn't cleared here.

---

## Verified facts

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Monet painted his water garden at Giverny for roughly the last three decades of his life (c. 1897–1926), returning to the same pond as the light changed. | Nelson-Atkins Museum, "Thirty years looking at a water garden": https://nelson-atkins.org/fpc/impressionism/632/ |
| F2 | As early as 1909 Monet described his wish for a decorated room of water-lily paintings intended to soothe and give refuge — the intention that became the Grandes Décorations. | Roger Marx, "Les 'Nymphéas' de M. Claude Monet," *Gazette des Beaux-Arts*, June 1909; discussed in Rutgers Art Review: https://rar.rutgers.edu/a-phenomenology-of-display-monets-lorangerie-the-panorama-rotunda-and-the-history-of-proto-installation-art-by-anthony-portulese/ |
| F3 | The Orangerie rooms are curved, encircling the visitor; the cycle was conceived as an environment without fixed viewpoint. | Rutgers Art Review (as F2); Musée de l'Orangerie. |

## Documented-intention quotes (cleared, with caveat)

**Caveat:** these are Monet's statements **as reported by Roger Marx** in the 1909
Gazette des Beaux-Arts article, and English translations vary between publications.
Reviewer: check the rendering against a published English translation before shipping;
always attribute as reported remarks, never as private writing.

### M1 — the endless whole (surface node)
> "…the illusion of an endless whole, of water without horizon or shore; nerves taut
> from overwork could have relaxed there…"

- Monet, describing the imagined room, as reported by Roger Marx, 1909 (as F2).

### M2 — the refuge (reflection node)
> "…a refuge of peaceful meditation in the center of a flowering aquarium."

- Same source and caveat as M1.
