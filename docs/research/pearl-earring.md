## Verified facts

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The painting is a *tronie* — a 17th-century Dutch study of a head/type — not a commissioned portrait; the girl's identity is unknown. | Mauritshuis research pages: https://www.mauritshuis.nl/en/our-collection/restoration-and-research/closer-to-vermeer-and-the-girl ; Frick/Mauritshuis: https://www.frick.org/exhibitions/mauritshuis/670 |
| F2 | No real pearl of that size is documented; the "pearl" was likely a glass drop varnished to shine like one. | Mauritshuis / Frick (as F1) |
| F3 | During the 1994 conservation it emerged that one of the highlights on the pearl was actually a flake of loosened paint; the restoration deepened the intimacy of the gaze. | Mauritshuis, "Vermeer illuminated": https://www.mauritshuis.nl/en/our-collection/restoration-and-research/closer-to-vermeer-and-the-girl/girl-with-a-blog/vermeer-illuminated |
| F4 | The dark background was originally a deep, enamel-like **green glaze** (indigo and weld) over black; those organic pigments have faded. | Mauritshuis (as F3) |
| F5 | The palette includes natural ultramarine (from lapis lazuli), ochres, lead white and bone/charcoal black; natural ultramarine was famously among the costliest pigments of the era. | Mauritshuis (as F3); Essential Vermeer catalog: https://www.essentialvermeer.com/catalogue/girl_with_a_pearl_earring.html |
